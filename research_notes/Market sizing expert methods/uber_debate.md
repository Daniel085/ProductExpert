# The 2014 Uber market-sizing debate (Damodaran vs. Gurley) and the "Big Market Delusion"

Scope: reconstruct both sides' actual methods and numbers so the exchange can serve as a worked example of top-down vs. bottom-up sizing of the same company, then test both against Uber's later reported figures. Conventions used throughout: **Gross Bookings / gross receipts / gross billings** = total value of rides (what riders pay); **revenue** = Uber's slice of that (its "take"); **value / valuation** = equity or enterprise value. Damodaran's 2014 base year was 2014 and his "year 10" was 2024, which makes a direct hindsight comparison possible.

## Q1. Damodaran's June 2014 top-down valuation: market figure, growth, share, resulting value

### Takeaway
Damodaran sized Uber top-down from the *existing* global taxi-and-limo market: $100B today, growing 6%/yr, Uber taking 10% of gross receipts and keeping 20% of that as revenue, at a 40% steady-state margin, which produced $6.6B of operating-asset value and $5.9B after a 10% failure haircut — versus the $17B VC pricing he was testing. Every link in that chain was explicit and tabulated, which is what made it so easy to attack and, later, to audit.

### Cited Findings
- Trigger: a June 2014 story that Uber had raised $1.2B at a $17B pre-money valuation; Damodaran judged $17B "far too high for a company that generates a few hundred million in revenues and has little to show in terms of operating income" — [Stern faculty page summarizing the post](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/faculty-research/how-do-you-value-company-uber.md); [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Market definition and size: "Global taxi market is $100 billion currently, expected to grow 6% a year for next ten years." Build: Tokyo $20–25B (USA Today, 2005), UK $14B (IBISWorld), US $11B (IBISWorld; about $3B of it New York), plus an *estimated* $50B for the rest of the world — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html); input box reproduced in [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Growth: 6%/yr for ten years (vs. historical taxi growth of about 2–3%), reaching about $183B in 2024 per the blog text; the reproduced spreadsheet row shows the year-10 market at $179,085M (the ~$4B gap is a rounding/compounding artifact between the prose and the table) — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html); [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Share: "Uber's market share of this market will increase to 10% over the next 10 years"; share path by year: 3.63%, 5.22%, 6.41%, 7.31%, 7.98%, 8.49%, 8.87%, 9.15%, 9.36%, 10.00%. He called 10% "at the optimistic end of the spectrum" — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf); [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Revenue slice: "Uber will keep 20% of the gross cab receipts as its revenues" (constant at 20% all ten years) — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Base-year (2014) figures he used: gross receipts $1.5B; revenues $300M; operating income $10M (assumed) — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Projected revenue path: $769M (yr 1) → $1,173M → $1,528M → $1,846M → $2,137M → $2,408M → $2,666M → $2,916M → $3,163M → $3,582M (yr 10 = 2024). Implied year-10 gross receipts = 10% x $179B ≈ $17.9B — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Margins and reinvestment: "I will assume that Uber's operating margin, in steady state, will be 40%" (margin ramps 7% → 40% over ten years); "I will assume that the sales to capital ratio for Uber will be 5.00, towards the high end of the spectrum" (i.e. $5 of incremental revenue per $1 of capital); tax rate 30% rising to 40% — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html); [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Risk: cost of capital 12% for years 1–5 ("top decile of US companies"), declining to 8% by year 10; terminal growth 2.5%, return on capital 25%, terminal value $14,418M; "I will assume that there is only a 10% chance of failure in Uber" with zero liquidation value — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Result: value of operating assets $6,595M; "Expected value = $6,595 (.9) = $5,895" — i.e. about $5.9B. Cash would be added and debt subtracted to get equity, "neither is likely to be large" — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf); [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Cross-checks he ran against $17B: 56.67x trailing revenue ($17B/$300M); 4.75x his year-10 revenue ($3.59B); social-media median EV/Sales 9.22x → ~$2.77B, Twitter's 21.82x → ~$6.5B; user-based ranges $1.05B–$8.5B; private VC-comparable median 32.14x revenue → just under $10B — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Break-even ("what you'd have to believe"): $17B requires a total market about three times his estimate (~$300B) or a share of 20%+; for the "disruption option" (package delivery, car rental, driverless) to add the missing ~$11B, those new markets would need to be at least four times the $100B base — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Named risks: city regulators/incumbents raising entry costs; deep-pocketed competitors (Lyft, Hailo) with no dominant service per a WSJ comparison; competition pushing the 20% slice to 10–15% or lower; cyclicality. Named upside: faster growth in under-served Asian/Latin American cities; the disruption option, which he saw as "possible but not probable", doubting Uber would keep a 20% cut in those markets — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Verdict quotes: "The combination of assumptions that you would need to get to $17 billion is improbable, at least in my view." and "The numbers seem to indicate that Uber is being overpriced by investors who have valued it at $17 billion." — [Damodaran, 9 June 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2014/06/a-disruptive-cab-ride-to-riches-uber.html)
- Later self-description of the 2014 narrative: "An urban, car-sharing company / With local (not global) networking benefits / Significant competitive advantages (and) / A low capital-intensity business model"; "Assuming that it would increase the size of the urban car service market by about 40%..., I valued Uber at just under $6 billion" — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf); [Damodaran, 12 Oct 2015](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/on-the-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-15-10-12)
- Gurley noted Damodaran had written "I have to confess that I just downloaded the app and have not used it yet" — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)

### Inferences
- The method is canonical top-down: external market estimate x growth x share x take-rate x margin, discounted. Half of the $100B "market" was itself an unsourced plug ($50B "rest of world"), so the anchor was weaker than its precision suggested.
- His own break-even analysis already located the two levers that mattered (market size ~3x or share ~2x); the debate that followed was entirely about those two inputs, not about the DCF mechanics.
- Base year 2014 → year 10 = 2024 makes his projections directly comparable with Uber's FY2024 reported figures (see Q4).

### Gaps
- The original spreadsheet file (xls) was linked from the post but not retrieved; the tabulated numbers above come from the reproduction in his Sept 2015 slides, which match the blog prose except the ~$179B vs ~$183B year-10 market figure.
- The FiveThirtyEight version (18 June 2014) was not fetched; its content is assumed to be a condensation of the blog post.

## Q2. Gurley's July 2014 reply: market expansion, bottom-up city evidence, elasticity and network effects

### Takeaway
Gurley accepted Damodaran's arithmetic but rejected the frame: the relevant market is not yesterday's taxi spend but the demand Uber creates by being cheaper, faster, safer and available in places taxis never served, plus a slice of the ~$6T car-ownership budget. Using city-level evidence (San Francisco's ~$120M taxi/limo market vs. Uber's much larger SF revenue) and explicit scenarios, he put the opportunity at roughly $450B–$1.3T, i.e. ~25x Damodaran's $10B (10% of $100B).

### Cited Findings
- Author/disclosure: Gurley (Benchmark) was Uber's Series A investor and a board member; published 11 July 2014 — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Core critique: Damodaran's $100B TAM assumes "the future will look quite like the past", i.e. that Uber has no effect on market size; and treating 10% as a ceiling (citing regulation/competition) ignores network effects that allow much higher share — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Argument 1, experience: Uber improves pick-up times, coverage, payment, civility and safety; "Most Uber riders believe they are safer in an Uber than in a traditional taxi." — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Argument 2, price elasticity: higher utilisation lets Uber cut prices and elasticity is "high": "lowering the price of car-for-hire transportation will increase the usage." — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Argument 3, network effects, with three mechanisms — pick-up times, coverage density, utilisation: "The more people that use Uber, the lower the overall price will be for the consumer." — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Argument 4, new use cases: suburban service; rental-car replacement (a $27B US market); couples' nights out; transporting kids and older parents; supplementing mass transit — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Bottom-up city evidence (San Francisco): Uber launched in 2010 with ~600 black cars; by Oct 2012 Kalanick reported 20% month-over-month growth; Kalanick's 2014 WSJ comments put historical SF taxi+limo spend at ~$120M and Uber's SF revenue "a healthy multiple bigger than that." Kalanick quote: "So it's not about the market that exists, it's about the market we're creating." — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Car-ownership build: ~1 billion cars worldwide (25% in the US); AAA annual ownership cost $9,000, cut by a third to $6,000 "for conservatism" → ~$6 trillion/yr; Uber-like services capturing 2.5%–12.5% → $150B–$750B — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Combined sizing: a 3x–6x expanded car-for-hire market plus the $150B–$750B ownership opportunity gives a TAM of about $450B to $1.3T; scenario table: in the bearish Scenario A Uber would need a 56% share, in the "more likely" Scenario G about 20%, to reach the implied outcomes; he says the scenarios make the market "25X that of Damadoran's $10B" — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/). Secondary sources render the range as $450B–$1.4T ([Firstbase, 8 Aug 2023](https://www.firstbase.io/reports/how-to-size-the-market-for-a-startup-idea)) or $450B–$1.3T ([Lynx Collective, 4 Feb 2026](https://lynxcollective.substack.com/p/escaping-the-tam-trap)).
- Framing of his own goal: "to present a reasonable and plausible argument", not to argue for a specific valuation; closing: Damodaran's math "simply does not hold up" and "This cannot be yesterday's market." — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Suggested bottom-up check: Damodaran could have asked heavy Uber users how their current spending compares with what they spent on taxis two years earlier — [Gurley, 11 July 2014](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Damodaran's own one-line summary of Gurley's counter: Uber was "More than just urban / Not just car service / On its way to creating global networking benefits" — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Contemporary commentary: Quartz framed it as "to get to $17 billion or more, you need to believe either that the market for car services is much bigger than Damodaran's estimate, or that Uber will be able to capture far more than 10% of it, or a mix of both" (snippet via search; page returned 403) — [Quartz, 2014](https://qz.com/218717/what-people-who-think-uber-is-worth-17-billion-believe); one blogger called it "a refreshingly civil back and forth ... that really highlights the different mindset of VC and value investors" — [noenthuda.com](https://www.noenthuda.com/tag/bill-gurley/)

### Inferences
- Gurley's post is "bottom-up" in two senses: (a) it starts from observed per-city behaviour (SF revenue vs the pre-existing taxi market) and from user-level substitution (rental cars, second cars), and (b) it builds the big number from a population x unit-cost x capture-rate chain rather than from an industry statistic. But the capture rates (2.5%–12.5% of car-ownership spend) are assumptions with no empirical anchor, so the top of his range is as much a plug as Damodaran's $50B rest-of-world.
- Neither man used the words "top-down" and "bottom-up"; the labels are later commentators' (and Damodaran's own 2019 self-description of his method as "top-down").
- The San Francisco datapoint is the strongest single piece of evidence in the exchange because it is a measured ratio (Uber revenue > historical market), not a forecast; Damodaran later cited a leaked report that Uber had made the SF market "three to four times larger" than the original taxi/limo market, effectively conceding the point.

### Gaps
- The task brief mentions a Gurley "resulting range ($25B–$130B)". The fetched article contains no such range; its outputs are TAM ranges ($150B–$750B ownership; ~$450B–$1.3T combined) and share requirements (56% / ~20%), not a valuation range. Treat "$25B–$130B" as unverified.
- The article does not mention "Innovator's Dilemma".
- Gurley's scenario table (Scenarios A–G) was described but the per-scenario numbers were not fully extracted by the fetch; only the 56% and ~20% share requirements are confirmed.

## Q3. Damodaran's responses (July 2014, Oct 2015) and the "Big Market Delusion" (2016 paper, 2019 post)

### Takeaway
Damodaran's immediate reply sorted Gurley's claims into possible / plausible / probable, accepted market expansion as plausible-to-probable, put a $2–3B option value on car ownership, and insisted network effects were local (city-by-city winners) rather than global; re-running his model on Gurley's narrative ($300B market, 40% share) gave $53.4B. By Oct 2015 he had moved his own inputs to a $230B "logistics" market, 25% share, 15% take, 25% margin and a $23.4B value, explicitly crediting Gurley. The Big Market Delusion work generalises the lesson: big-market stories plus founder/VC overconfidence plus pricing-not-valuing produce collective overpricing, and the honest test is to sum what every player's price implies and compare it with the market.

### Cited Findings
**July 2014 reply**
- Three reasons Gurley "has the advantage over mine": insider knowledge of Uber's workings and test-market performance; "skin in the game"; a track record of being right on young-company investments — [Damodaran, 16 July 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/possible-plausible-and-probable-big-14-07-16)
- Framework: "not everything that is possible is plausible," and not all plausible outcomes become probable. Broadening the car-service market (light users, non-users, suburbs, rental substitution) is a candidate for "probable" but must clear three barriers: a reason to switch, inertia, and the status quo. Suburban and mass-transit users may have moved from plausible to probable on Gurley's test-market data — "possible but unverified" from outside — [Damodaran, 16 July 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/possible-plausible-and-probable-big-14-07-16)
- Car ownership: a "possibility" for both; Damodaran attaches an option value of about $2–3B because it is distant and Uber's model there is unformed; he also challenges the "young people own fewer cars" trend as possibly cyclical and notes a car is more than transport — [Damodaran, 16 July 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/possible-plausible-and-probable-big-14-07-16)
- Network effects: Gurley's three mechanisms (pick-up times, coverage density, utilisation) are *local*; local effects imply city-by-city fights with different winners ("Uber in San Francisco, Lyft in Chicago, a new firm in London"); global advantages (data, partnerships with card/phone/car companies) look weaker and must become central for Gurley's case to hold — [Damodaran, 16 July 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/possible-plausible-and-probable-big-14-07-16)
- Posture: "a cautious convert"; more interested in what drives Uber's value than in proving it overpriced; cites Kahneman that "experience is not a very good teacher"; his caution "may make me a bad venture capitalist" — [Damodaran, 16 July 2014](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/possible-plausible-and-probable-big-14-07-16)
- Side-by-side narratives (table from his Sept 2015 deck): Gurley narrative — "Uber will expand the car service market substantially, bringing in mass transit users & non-users from the suburbs into the market, and use its networking advantage to gain a dominant market share, while maintaining its revenue slice at 20%": total market $300B growing 3%/yr, share 40%, slice 20% → value $53.4B + option value of entering car ownership ($10B+). Damodaran narrative: $100B growing 6%, share 10%, slice 20% → $5.9B + option value $2–3B — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf). In prose he says he "revalued Uber on that narrative at $54 billion" — [Damodaran, 12 Oct 2015](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/on-the-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-15-10-12)

**October 2015 revaluation**
- "I will remain grateful to Bill Gurley for a post where he took me to task for having too narrow a vision of Uber's business model." — [Damodaran, 12 Oct 2015](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/on-the-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-15-10-12); also quoted at [Stern, 11 Oct 2015](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/news/op-ed-professor-aswath-damodaran-identifies-several-factors-influenced-his-current-valuation-uber.md)
- Evidence that moved him: ride sharing reached exurbia/suburbia; a leaked report that Uber had made the SF market "three to four times larger" than the original taxi/limo market; expansion into logistics (Hong Kong, New York) and food delivery (Los Angeles); India and China as the big growth markets; leaked gross billings "climbing to over $10 billion in 2015"; NYC taxi revenues and medallion prices falling — [Damodaran, 12 Oct 2015](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/on-the-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-15-10-12); [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Offsetting evidence: regional incumbents (Ola, Didi Kuaidi, GrabTaxi) dominate their markets; driver sign-up bonuses surging; California Labor Commission ruling that drivers are employees → "semi-employees"; insurance and legal costs; contribution margins in leaked data ranged 3.5% (Seattle) to 11.1% (Stockholm, Johannesburg); 2014 operating loss greater than revenues — [Damodaran, 12 Oct 2015](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/on-the-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-15-10-12); [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Revised inputs, June 2014 → Sept 2015: Total market $100B "Urban car service" → $230B "Logistics"; growth "increase market size by 34%; CAGR 6%" → "Double market size; CAGR 10.39%" (market reaches $618B in year 10); market share 10% (local networking) → 25% (weak global networking); slice of gross receipts 20% → 15% (fading from 20% to 15% over ten years); operating margin 40% (low-cost model) → 25% (partial-employee model); cost of capital 12% → 10%; probability of failure 10% → 0%; value of equity $5.9B → $23.4B — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- Sept 2015 projection: base gross billings $10,840M (2015) rising to $154,513M in 2025; revenue $2,168M → $23,177M; operating margin −23% → +25%; value of operating assets $23,429M; compared with "the $51 billion value that VCs are attaching to the company today" — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf); [Damodaran, 12 Oct 2015](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/on-the-uber-rollercoaster-narrative-15-10-12)
- His menu of market definitions and share levels (useful as a sizing ladder): A1 Urban car service $100B; A2 All car service $175B; A3 Logistics $230B; A4 Mobility services $310B. Share: no network effects 5%; weak local 10%; strong local 15%; weak global 25%; strong global 40%. Slice of gross receipts by competitive advantage: none 5%, weak 10%, semi-strong 15%, strong & sustainable 20%. Margin by expense profile: independent contractor 40%, partial employee 25%, full employee 15% — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)
- "If you think value is too low, don't blame the DCF model for my cramped imagination. It must be because you think that my total market is too small, that Uber will be more dominant in these markets and that the costs will start to adjust as business scales up." — [UberSept2015.pdf](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/blog/UberSept2015.pdf)

**The Big Market Delusion (Cornell & Damodaran)**

Earlier SSRN working-paper version (abstract id 2831855, 2016) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2831855) (403 on fetch; existence confirmed by search)
- Abstract (verbatim excerpts): "Big markets draw in entrepreneurs and VCs, overconfident that they can conquer these markets, which leads to a collective overpricing and an inevitable correction." Big markets offer "the promise of easily scalable revenues"; the paper examines "the role of overconfidence on the part of both entrepreneurs and their financiers" via three cases: "one in which the process has fully played out (1990s dot-com retail)", "one in which it has been unfolding for a while (online advertising)", "one in which it is just beginning (the cannabis market)"; "We suggest several lessons for investors, regulators, and businesses" — [CFA Institute FAJ page](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2020/0015198x-2020-1730655)
- Secondary summary of the paper's valuation points: "pricing based upon multiples and the peer group can lead to significant overpricing"; detailed valuations, "flawed though they might be, can prove to be the first step in recognizing the big market delusion, and perhaps correcting for it"; big markets are not proprietary, so a large TAM does not by itself support a high valuation since competition captures much of it — [BVResources summary](https://www.bvresources.com/articles/bvwire/the-big-market-delusion-overvalues-companies-at-first) (via search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch)
- Damodaran's 30 Dec 2019 restatement — three ingredients: (1) a big market, where "the extrapolation that the change would lead [to] huge and profitable markets was made casually"; (2) overconfidence — Kahneman's "mother of all behavioral biases", with entrepreneurs and VCs "among the most over confident amongst us"; (3) a pricing game — young companies "tend to be priced, not valued", on raw metrics like users or revenue; followed by "an inevitable correction that brings the pricing back to earth" — [Damodaran, 30 Dec 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-the-19-12-30)
- Evidence, dot-com retail: 295 internet IPOs in 1999, "more than 60% of all initial public offerings that year"; the Bloomberg Internet Index rose 250% in 1999 and peaked near $2.9T in early 2000 while revenues were "a fraction of that value"; of public internet retailers in March 2000, "more than two-thirds failed"; Amazon lost ~90% of its value — [Damodaran, 30 Dec 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-the-19-12-30)
- Evidence, online advertising (the aggregation test): using 2015 prices, Facebook's market cap implied 2025 revenues of $129,318M (93% from ads); all public online-ad companies together implied ~$523B of 2025 revenue versus an estimated 2025 online-ad market of ~$466B — "being overpriced relative to the market"; by 2019 the breakeven 2029 revenue was $573B, read as the market "starting to take account of the limits", a correction "in slow motion" — [Damodaran, 30 Dec 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-the-19-12-30)
- Evidence, cannabis: forecasts of $70B (conservative) to ~$150B (optimistic) global sales by 2024; top-ten companies' combined market cap "in excess of $48 billion", >20x collective revenue; Tilray >$13B on ~$28M revenue; within a year the group lost "more than 50%" (Tilray −80.7%, Canopy −38.6%) — [Damodaran, 30 Dec 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-the-19-12-30)
- Ride sharing in that post: Uber "estimated its TAM in excess of $6 trillion"; Uber and Lyft are cited as transportation-theme momentum that corrected after IPO. The post does not revisit the 2014 Gurley exchange — [Damodaran, 30 Dec 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-the-19-12-30)
- Prescriptions: founders/VCs should test market-size and business-model assumptions with people outside their bubble, not dismiss contrary data as aberrations, keep cash buffers and avoid lock-ins like debt, and if forced to choose between growth metrics and a profitable business, "pick the latter"; public investors can ride the theme (timing risk) or short a basket; regulators should not try to eliminate bubbles because they spur innovation — [Damodaran, 30 Dec 2019](https://aswathdamodaran.substack.com/p/the-market-is-huge-revisiting-the-19-12-30)
- Secondhand statistic attributed to the paper: 80% of entrepreneurs believe they have at least a 70% chance of succeeding while ~75% of start-ups fail — [Macro Hive](https://macrohive.com/deep-dives/how-start-ups-and-vcs-get-fooled-by-the-big-market-delusion) (paywalled; via search snippet, unverified against the paper)

### Inferences
- The honest "big market" method implied by this body of work is: (1) define the market at several nested levels (his $100B/$175B/$230B/$310B ladder) and state which one you are using; (2) map share to a named mechanism (no / local / global network effects → 5–40%); (3) tie take-rate to competitive advantage and margin to the cost model; (4) back out what the current price implies for revenue and run the aggregation test (sum of implied revenues across all players vs the market); (5) treat adjacent markets as options with explicit probabilities rather than folding them into TAM.
- Between June 2014 and Sept 2015 Damodaran raised his market by 2.3x and share by 2.5x but cut take-rate (20→15%) and margin (40→25%); the net was ~4x on value. That is the classic shape of a corrected top-down model: the market-size error was partly offset by profitability being worse than the original story.
- The Big Market Delusion is the mirror image of Gurley's critique: Gurley says top-down from the existing market under-sizes a market-creating product; Cornell–Damodaran say a huge TAM, valued company by company, over-sizes the aggregate because everyone assumes they win.

- The task brief refers to a Damodaran post titled "Up, up and away! A crowd-valuation of Uber". No post by that title was found; the July 2014 reply is "Possible, Plausible and Probable". The Sept 2015 deck has a slide "Uber: The Narrative Effect in December 2014" (image only), which may be the referent. Treat the title as unverified.

Whether the paper itself contains a ride-sharing aggregate-valuation example (Uber+Lyft+Didi+Ola vs taxi market) could not be confirmed.

## Q4. Hindsight: Uber's S-1 TAM claim, actual gross bookings / revenue / market cap, and which assumptions held

### Takeaway
On market size Gurley was directionally right by a wide margin: Uber's 2024 gross bookings ($162.8B, all segments) were ~9x Damodaran's projected 2024 Uber gross receipts (~$18B) and nearly as large as his projection for the *entire* 2024 global taxi market (~$180B), and its market cap (~$196–212B in late 2025) is ~30x his $5.9B. But the profitability side vindicated Damodaran's caution: Uber lost money for a decade, its first GAAP operating profit came in 2023, and about half of today's bookings come from food delivery, which neither side's 2014 TAM included. Uber's own 2019 S-1 TAM ($5.7T, 7% of world GDP) was widely dismissed as an unserviceable theoretical maximum.

### Inferences
- Scorecard on market size (Damodaran's own year-10 = 2024): projected 2024 Uber gross receipts ≈ $17.9B and revenue ≈ $3.6B vs actual FY2024 Gross Bookings $162.8B and revenue $44.0B — misses of ~9x and ~12x. Mobility-only bookings (Q4 2024 $22.8B → ~$90B annualised, my calculation) alone were ~5x his projected Uber total and about half of what he projected the *whole* global taxi market to be in 2024 (~$180B). So "Uber expands the market" was not just true but dominant.
- Gurley's TAM range ($450B–$1.3T) has not been reached by Uber: FY2025 Gross Bookings of $193B are 15–43% of that range, and his "car ownership substitution" tranche ($150B–$750B) is not separately observable in Uber's reporting. His frame was right; his upper magnitudes remain unproven a decade on.
- Damodaran's Sept 2015 revision ($230B logistics market, 25% share → 2025 gross billings $154.5B, revenue $23.2B, value $23.4B) undershot actual 2025 Gross Bookings ($193.5B) by ~20% and revenue ($52.0B) by >2x. Part of the revenue gap is accounting (take-rate as reported revenue/Gross Bookings was 22.6% in 2018 and ~26.9% in 2025, versus his assumed fade to 15%; Uber's reported revenue also includes some principal-basis and advertising items that are not a pure "slice"), so compare bookings, not revenue, when testing the market-size claim.
- What Damodaran got right: (a) network effects proved largely local/regional — Uber ceded China, Southeast Asia and Russia to Didi, Grab and Yandex, consistent with his "city-by-city winners" argument and with Gurley's bearish Scenario A needing 56% share; (b) the 20% take-rate came under pressure and margins were far below 40% — operating income was negative until 2023 and FY2025 operating margin is ~10.7% of revenue / ~2.9% of Gross Bookings (my calculation from the release), closer to his 2015 "partial employee" 25%-of-revenue scenario than to 40%; (c) the valuation levels he was reacting to ($17B in 2014, $51B in 2015) were, in fact, exceeded within five years, so his calls that those prices were too high were wrong even as several of his operating assumptions were right.
- What neither side modelled: delivery. Q4 2025 Delivery Gross Bookings ($25.4B) are 93% of Mobility ($27.4B); Damodaran listed "package delivery" only as an unpriced option, and Gurley's TAM was all passenger transport. The largest single hindsight lesson for sizing is therefore not top-down vs bottom-up but that adjacent-market optionality can end up as half the business.
- Uber's S-1 TAM is a third sizing style — "miles travelled x all transport spend" — and shows the failure mode the Big Market Delusion describes: a market so large (7% of world GDP) that the share needed to justify any price looks trivially small ("<1% penetration"), which removes the discipline the share assumption is supposed to provide.
