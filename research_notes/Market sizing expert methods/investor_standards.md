# Investor-side standards for TAM/SAM/SOM market sizing

Scope: how venture investors and startup advisors expect founders to build and present market sizings, and what they reject. Compiled 2026-10-09. Primary sources were fetched directly where possible; quotes marked "verbatim" were extracted from the fetched page. Where only a search snippet or a secondary summary was available, that is stated. Vendor/content-marketing sources were avoided except where noted.

Research-process note for the writer: several firm sites were unreachable or had removed content (Sequoia's current page is reduced to one sentence; YC library pages 404 on fetch; Greylock's Medium post returned 503; Sam Altman's startupclass site no longer resolves). See Gaps in each section.

## Key Question 1: How do the named firms/people define TAM/SAM/SOM and describe how to build them?

### Takeaway
There is no single investor-side definition; the most-cited firms converge on "TAM = count of real target customers × what each pays per year", treat SAM/SOM as optional refinements (several explicitly disparage the three-bubble SAM/SOM funnel), and say the market should be sized bottom-up with top-down used only as a sanity check. Sequoia's original template is the odd one out: it literally asks for "TAM (top down), SAM (bottoms up) and SOM".

### Cited Findings

**Sequoia Capital**
- Sequoia's original "Writing a Business Plan" outline (originally at sequoiacap.com/grove/posts/6bzx/writing-a-business-plan), as reproduced slide-for-slide by PitchDeckCoach, gives the Market Size slide as exactly two bullets (verbatim): "Identify/profile the customer you cater to" and "Calculate the TAM (top down), SAM (bottoms up) and SOM". The preceding "Why Now" slide reads: "Set-up the historical evolution of your category" / "Define recent trends that make your solution possible". — [Sequoia Capital Pitch Deck Template, PitchDeckCoach reproduction (PDF)](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1126518382322406/Sequoia%20Capital_pitchdecktemplate.pdf)
- Sequoia's current page has been cut down; the entire market section now reads (verbatim): "Identify your customer and your market. Some of the best companies invent their own markets." No mention of TAM/SAM/SOM or top-down/bottom-up remains. — [Sequoia Capital, "Writing a Business Plan"](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)

**Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)**
- "16 More Startup Metrics" (Anu Hariharan, Frank Chen, Jeff Jordan, Sept 23, 2015) has a dedicated TAM entry. Verbatim: "TAM is a way to quantify the market size/ opportunity." and "we like seeing a bottoms-up analysis, which takes into account your target customer profile" (their willingness to pay, and how the product will be marketed and sold). "By contrast, a top-down analysis calculates TAM based on market share and a total market size." — [a16z, "16 More Startup Metrics"](https://a16z.com/16-more-startup-metrics/)
- a16z's worked example: top-down toothbrush TAM = 1.36B people × $1 × 40% share = $540M/yr, which they reject because "This analysis not only tends to overstate market size (why 40%?)" and ignores the difficulty of distribution; the bottom-up version counts sales through actual channels (drugstores, grocery, online) and forces the founder to think about the sales/marketing team needed. — [a16z, "16 More Startup Metrics"](https://a16z.com/16-more-startup-metrics/)
- Verbatim: "It is important not to 'game' the TAM number when pitching investors." and "many of the best internet companies sought to address what appeared to be modest TAMs in the beginning." with "Take eBay (collectibles and antiques) and Airbnb (rooms in other people's places)" as examples; and "using the size of an existing market might actually understate the opportunity of new business models". — [a16z, "16 More Startup Metrics"](https://a16z.com/16-more-startup-metrics/)

**Y Combinator / Paul Graham / Sam Altman**
- No YC Library page on market sizing could be fetched (see Gaps). The YC-adjacent canonical framing is Paul Graham's "well" metaphor (Nov 2012), verbatim: "you can either build something a large number of people want a small amount, or something a small number of people want a large amount. Choose the latter." and "You can either dig a hole that's broad but shallow, or one that's narrow and deep, like a well." "Microsoft was a well when they made Altair Basic." "Microcomputers seemed like toys when Apple and Microsoft started working on them." — [Paul Graham, "How to Get Startup Ideas"](https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)
- Sam Altman's CS183B Lecture 1 (Stanford, 2014) is summarized by a secondary source as: start in a small market with an idea nobody else is working on, "attack the small market of the target user and gradually expand", and judge by "how much the market will grow in the future", aiming for one that "may become a huge market within 10 years"; current market size is said to be irrelevant to his investment decisions. Secondary paraphrase, not verbatim. — [Gigazine summary of Altman lecture 1, Oct 22, 2014](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20141022-sam-altman-lecture)
- Kevin Hale's YC Startup School 2019 lecture "How to Evaluate Startup Ideas" lists, per YC's own podcast page, market criteria of "a market growing 20% a year", a 10x-better product, and monopoly potential; the official page confirms the outline but not the bottom-up method. — [YC blog, Startup School podcast preview with Kevin Hale](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-podcast-preview-with-kevin-hale)

**Point Nine (Christoph Janz)**
- Janz's "Five ways to build a $100 million business" (Oct 5, 2014) is the canonical customers × ARPA ladder (verbatim): "1,000 enterprise customers paying you $100k+ per year each" (elephants); "10,000 medium-sized companies paying you $10k+ per year each" (deer); "100,000 small businesses paying you $1k+ per year each" (rabbits); "1 million consumers or 'prosumers' paying you $100+ per year each" (mice); "10 million active consumers who you monetize at $10+ per year each by selling ads" (flies). He calls these "gross simplification[s]" dependent on conversion and churn. — [Christoph Janz, The Angel VC](http://christophjanz.blogspot.com/2014/10/five-ways-to-build-100-million-business.html)
- Funnel multipliers from the same post: deer need "100,000 or more leads"; rabbits need "something in the order of 0.5-2 million trial signups"; mice need "at least 10-20 million people to try your application"; flies need "roughly 100 million people who download your app or use your website". — [Christoph Janz, The Angel VC](http://christophjanz.blogspot.com/2014/10/five-ways-to-build-100-million-business.html)

**Pear VC**
- "Market Sizing Guide" (Pear Team, Aug 3, 2021; secondary sources credit Ian Taylor). Verbatim: "Multiply the number of customers by the average revenue per customer per year." "Ideally you will only use top-down to sanity check the magnitude of your bottom-up estimate." "There is no single definition of market size." "many investors do not know the exact definitions either" (so state your methodology). "Present bottom-up estimates for your total addressable market and for your revenue in 5 years." Venture bar: show potential to "capture at least hundreds of millions of dollars of high-margin revenue" within a decade inside a "multiple billion dollar market." Pricing rule of thumb: "you can charge in the range of 10-30% of the value you create for your customer." The guide calls the textbook top-down funnel (largest market → narrow → assumed share) "unconvincing". — [Pear VC, "Market Sizing Guide"](https://pear.vc/market-sizing-guide/)

**Underscore VC (Richard Dulude, Venture Partner)**
- Verbatim: "Bottom-up market sizing = [# of Customers] * [Price They're Willing to Pay]". Top-down is "Pulling existing data from analysts like Gartner or Forrester and estimating a market's value" and "can be directionally helpful" but "it rarely predicts your specific market's true nature." "To estimate your market size, take a bottom-up approach." Steps: identify the specific customer, count how many exist, estimate how many you can reach, set the price, estimate how many will buy (the last is "the most challenging"). — [Underscore VC, "Bottom-Up Market Size Slide"](https://underscore.vc/resources/bottom-up-market-size-slide/)

**Dreamit Ventures (Charles LaCalle, May 16, 2019)**
- Formula attributed to Steve Barsh: "(# of potential customers) x (price) = TAM", built from firsthand company data. Top-down is a founder's guess at a small share of an "invariably massive pie" that relies on opaque analyst reports and ignores pricing and the startup's ability to change market size. — [Dreamit, "How to Create the Perfect TAM Slide for VC Pitches"](https://www.dreamit.com/journal/how-to-create-the-perfect-tam-slide-for-vc-pitches)

**Octopus Ventures (Edward Keelan, Jan 19, 2022)**
- Defines TAM as "the total number of customers a company can possibly win within its ideal customer profile, multiplied by the price it can charge." Recommends a single addressable-market number rather than a SAM/SOM funnel; calls SAM/SOM "somewhat academic"; "in isolation, TAM isn't a useful metric for investors." — [Octopus Ventures, "We need to talk about TAM"](https://octopusventures.com/blog/we-need-to-talk-about-tam/)

**Jason Lemkin / SaaStr**
- Lemkin's post "Every Year, You Should Double Your TTAM. Your True TAM." defines TTAM as "the real TAM you are selling into" and argues founders should double it yearly; "almost always, you'll exhaust your initial wedge." Examples: Salesloft "started off as a pretty basic prospecting and then email cadence tool" and became a full revenue suite; Talkdesk began as "an SMB Zendesk and Salesforce plug in" and became enterprise contact center; Algolia accelerated after $100m ARR by moving upmarket. He says investors "don't want to worry too much about TAM" past a certain point. (Page undated; embedded tweet dated May 31, 2025.) — [SaaStr, "Every Year You Should Double Your TTAM"](https://www.saastr.com/every-year-you-should-double-your-ttam-your-true-tam/)

**Fusion VC with PSG (Guy Katsovich; expert Rotem Shacham, Director at PSG; July 2, 2023)**
- TAM: "an estimation of the total sales you could achieve within a specific target market"; SAM: subset you can realistically serve; SOM: subset of SAM capturable in a timeframe. Shacham advises against leaning on SAM/SOM: "You're just adding more and more layers of assumptions and hypotheticals." Bottom-up formula given as average price × number of customers × penetration rate; top-down via IT-spend share and CAGR. Run both: if "in the same ballpark" it is reasonable; if "radically different", an error is likely. — [Fusion VC, "Market Sizing: Make Assumptions But Don't Make S@##! Up"](https://blog.fusion-vc.com/p/masterclass-market-sizing)

**TechCrunch advisor column (Marjorie Radlo-Zandi, angel/advisor, Mar 9, 2022)**
- TAM "describes total revenues within a larger sector"; SAM is the segment your product serves; SOM is "serviceable obtainable market, or market share." Says investors look for all three and care most about SOM. This piece is a general explainer; it does not give a bottom-up formula. — [TechCrunch, "How to calculate your startup's TAM, SAM and SOM"](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/09/how-to-calculate-your-startups-tam-sam-and-som/)

**Lenny Rachitsky**
- The only Lenny's Newsletter piece located that touches market sizing is the paywalled "How to identify your ideal customer profile (ICP)" (Aug 29, 2023, Part 3 of a B2B series); the visible portion advises getting "super-specific and super-narrow with your ICP" and picking three attributes. A third-party summary claims it says to define the ICP before sizing the market so bottom-up math is credible; that is not verified against the paywalled text. — [Lenny's Newsletter, ICP post](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-identify-your-ideal-customer?r=7fhav)

### Inferences
- The practitioner consensus formula is TAM = (number of customers in the ideal customer profile) × (annual revenue per customer), with optional frequency/penetration multipliers (Fusion VC adds penetration; Pear notes ARPA may itself be transactions × price). SAM/SOM are treated by Pear, Octopus, Underscore and PSG as either redundant or as places where founders hide assumptions.
- Sequoia's "TAM top down, SAM bottoms up" wording is the likely origin of the widespread "show both" convention; third-party reproductions of this template are everywhere, so a method doc should cite the reproduction and note the current Sequoia page no longer carries it.

## Key Question 2: What is the canonical statement of "bottom-up, not top-down", who said it best, and what formula do they give?

### Takeaway
The clearest early firm-level statement is a16z's 2015 "16 More Startup Metrics" (bottoms-up preferred; top-down "tends to overstate market size (why 40%?)"); the most quotable formulas are Underscore's "[# of Customers] * [Price They're Willing to Pay]", Pear's "number of customers × average revenue per customer per year" with top-down "only... to sanity check", and Dreamit/Steve Barsh's "(# of potential customers) x (price) = TAM". Bill Gurley's 2014 Uber essay is the canonical demonstration of why top-down from an existing market fails.

### Cited Findings
- a16z (2015): "we like seeing a bottoms-up analysis, which takes into account your target customer profile"; top-down "tends to overstate market size (why 40%?)" and ignores how hard it is to reach buyers; bottom-up forces a concrete view of the sales/marketing team required. — [a16z, "16 More Startup Metrics"](https://a16z.com/16-more-startup-metrics/)
- Pear VC (2021): "Multiply the number of customers by the average revenue per customer per year." "Ideally you will only use top-down to sanity check the magnitude of your bottom-up estimate." — [Pear VC](https://pear.vc/market-sizing-guide/)
- Underscore VC: "Bottom-up market sizing = [# of Customers] * [Price They're Willing to Pay]"; bottom-up means "Proving your market customer by customer". — [Underscore VC](https://underscore.vc/resources/bottom-up-market-size-slide/)
- Dreamit (2019): "(# of potential customers) x (price) = TAM" (Steve Barsh); top-down relies on "opaque analyst reports" and "often don't assume that a startup can change the actual size of the TAM"; Uber cited as a case where top-down "dramatically undersized the market" while bottom-up data from the first city would have shown expansion. — [Dreamit](https://www.dreamit.com/journal/how-to-create-the-perfect-tam-slide-for-vc-pitches)
- Fusion VC/PSG (2023): bottom-up = average price × number of potential customers × expected penetration rate; the piece says top-down "may be more reliable at early stages" and bottom-up "becomes more useful once you have sales data" (a minority view relative to the others), and recommends running both as a cross-check. — [Fusion VC](https://blog.fusion-vc.com/p/masterclass-market-sizing)
- Octopus Ventures (2022): "the total number of customers a company can possibly win within its ideal customer profile, multiplied by the price it can charge." — [Octopus Ventures](https://octopusventures.com/blog/we-need-to-talk-about-tam/)
- Reach Capital's Jomayra Herrera (2023): "You want to do bottom-up, you want to do top-down, [and] you want to do a ton of market research to triangulate." — [TechCrunch, "How founders should approach TAM when venture capital is scarce"](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/06/how-founders-should-approach-tam-when-venture-capital-is-scarce/)
- Bill Gurley (July 11, 2014), responding to Aswath Damodaran's Uber valuation built on a $100B historical taxi/limo TAM and a 10% share cap: "The past can be a poor guide for the future if the future offering is materially different than the past." He calls Damodaran's TAM assumption "a critical error", rebuilds the market from (a) the historic market being "3-6X bigger" where Uber has liquidity (San Francisco evidence) and (b) car-ownership substitution: "One billion global cars multiplied by a $6,000 annual cost of ownership" × 2.5-12.5% substitution, giving a combined TAM of roughly $450B-$1.3T, versus Damodaran's $100B. — [Bill Gurley, "How to Miss By a Mile"](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Marc Andreessen (June 25, 2007) on why market size matters at all: "market is the most important factor in a startup's success or failure"; "In a great market—a market with lots of real potential customers—the market pulls product out of the startup." Cites Andy Rachleff: "The #1 company-killer is lack of market." — [pmarca, "The only thing that matters"](https://pmarchive.com/guide_to_startups_part4.html)

### Inferences
- The composite formula practitioners use is: TAM = Σ over segments of (count of ICP accounts) × (annual price/ACV), where ACV may be decomposed as units (seats/transactions) × price × frequency; SOM, if shown, = that × a penetration assumption that is stated and justified. No single source gives all three multipliers in one line; Fusion VC/PSG is the closest (price × customers × penetration).
- "Who said it best": for a short founder-facing rule, Pear's "only use top-down to sanity check" is the crispest; for an investor-side rationale, a16z's "why 40%?" and the Gurley essay are the ones most worth preserving.

### Gaps
- No Sequoia, YC, Bessemer, or First Round primary text with a bottom-up formula was retrievable. The frequently repeated "YC says bottom-up" claim is unverified.

## Key Question 3: What failure modes do investors call out?

### Takeaway
Named investors repeatedly mock: the "X% of a huge market" slide, analyst-report (Gartner/Forrester) TAMs pasted without derivation, sizing the problem or the adjacent industry instead of the ICP, trillion-dollar TAMs, false precision, counting buyers without a price basis, and TAM-expansion claims with no evidence. Wrong-unit (seats vs companies) and "TAM at today's price" errors are discussed only indirectly in the sources found.

### Cited Findings

**"1% / 2% of a huge market"**
- Bill Reichert (Pegasus Tech Ventures, Nov 17, 2022): "The fault lies in the pitch coaching industry that insists that every deck include a slide with TAM, SAM and SOM." "When this slide appears, most investors chuckle (or weep)." "Not only are the numbers always exaggerated, they are also irrelevant." He mocks the assumption that "2% of a very large market" beats "20% of a smaller market", trillion-dollar TAMs ("$2.5 trillion"), and false precision such as "CAGR of 17.65%". His parody example: ~90M US dogs × $9.95/month = "$10 billion market". — [TechCrunch, "TAM takedown"](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2443773)
- Felicis Ventures' Aydin Senkut (2022): "With TAM, it is almost guaranteed you're going to be wrong." "If that number is not correlated to a real calculation or real thinking, I do think that that is a red flag." "The insight behind it is more important than the absolute calculation." — [TechCrunch, "What investors really think about the TAM slide"](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/04/what-investors-really-think-about-the-tam-slide-in-your-pitch-deck/)
- Lux Capital's Deena Shakir (2022): "We can tell if a founder has just done some calculations and got excited about a TAM"; TAM "tells us not necessarily about the business or its future"; "What's much more important is the founder-market fit and the grit and the resilience of that founder". — [TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/04/what-investors-really-think-about-the-tam-slide-in-your-pitch-deck/)
- Secondary investor-adjacent commentary names the "1% market fallacy" and describes the stock slide ("a $100 billion TAM from a research report, a neat arrow pointing down to 'just 1%'"). These are Substack/advisory sources, not firm blogs. — [The Venture Crew, "Is the 1% Market Fallacy Holding Your Startup Back?"](https://theventurecrew.substack.com/p/is-the-1-market-fallacy-holding-your); [n47, "Why 'All we need is 1%...' is a red flag"](https://www.n47.com/insights/why-all-we-need-is-1-percent-of-this-very-large-market-is-a-red-flag)

**Analyst-report TAM pasted from Gartner/IDC/Forrester**
- Underscore VC: analyst figures are "often arbitrary and hard to explain, since the founder didn't do the research"; "Founders often throw in TAM, SAM, and SOM just to add something"; what matters is "How did you get there?" — [Underscore VC](https://underscore.vc/resources/bottom-up-market-size-slide/)
- Dreamit: relying on outside market reports "looks lazy and can be wrong or taken out of context". — [Dreamit](https://www.dreamit.com/journal/how-to-create-the-perfect-tam-slide-for-vc-pitches)
- Index Ventures' Jahanvi Sardana (July 2025): over-reliance on industry reports "can suggest the founder hasn't deeply thought through the market they're building in"; a TAM slide is fine "if you explain the math behind it"; present your own insight rather than industry metrics. — [TechCrunch, Sardana](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/25/index-ventures-jahanvi-sardana-shares-the-truth-about-tam-and-what-founders-should-focus-on-instead/)
- Cake Ventures' Monique Woodard (2023): citing a single analyst report is not enough; she would rather hear "I don't know" than unsupported claims. — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/06/how-founders-should-approach-tam-when-venture-capital-is-scarce/)

**Sizing the problem / the adjacent industry instead of the ICP**
- Octopus Ventures: "The key error founders make is to overstate their TAM by highlighting the problem." — [Octopus Ventures](https://octopusventures.com/blog/we-need-to-talk-about-tam/)
- Fusion VC/PSG: "Wrong market" (claiming all e-commerce when you sell recommendation software); "Trillion-dollar TAMs: very few markets are actually ever going to reach this level"; but also "A hyper-specific segment is an impenetrable glass ceiling" (a market capped at $300M cannot support a $1B company). — [Fusion VC](https://blog.fusion-vc.com/p/masterclass-market-sizing)
- Dreamit: "Overly broad customer counts" (every potential buyer instead of the served segment); "Missing geography"; "Unjustified pricing" (be ready to show your work, especially pre-revenue/enterprise). — [Dreamit](https://www.dreamit.com/journal/how-to-create-the-perfect-tam-slide-for-vc-pitches)
- Reichert: vague customer definitions such as "dog owners" show no real research; "Most investors want to see market opportunity, not market size." — [TechCrunch, "TAM takedown"](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2443773)

**Confusing spend today with addressable spend; TAM at today's price; ignoring non-consumption**
- Gurley: Damodaran's error was anchoring to historical taxi spend; Uber is "achieving liquidity in geographic regions where consumers rarely order taxis" (suburbs, kids' transport, rental-car replacement), elasticity is "high" so lower prices expand usage, and "Gaining market share puts them in a better position to gain more market share". — [Gurley](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- a16z: "using the size of an existing market might actually understate the opportunity of new business models". — [a16z](https://a16z.com/16-more-startup-metrics/)
- Dreamit: top-down "does not allow a nuanced inclusion of factors such as pricing and different pricing models". — [Dreamit](https://www.dreamit.com/journal/how-to-create-the-perfect-tam-slide-for-vc-pitches)
- Chris Dixon (Jan 3, 2010): "The next big thing will start out looking like a toy"; "Disruptive technologies are dismissed as toys because when they are first launched they 'undershoot' user needs" (Christensen), with external forces such as "microchips getting cheaper, bandwidth becoming ubiquitous" closing the gap. — [cdixon.org](https://cdixon.org/2010/01/03/the-next-big-thing-will-start-out-looking-like-a-toy)

**"TAM expansion" stories without evidence**
- Felicis (Senkut/Faga session preview, Mar 2022): most investors aren't interested "unless that number starts with a B", but founders must "craft a clear, realistic plan that maps out how you'll advance from selling to your 'wedge' toward larger adjacent groups." — [TechCrunch, Felicis "from wedge to TAM"](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/09/felicis-ventures-aydin-senkut-and-gp-viviana-faga-take-you-from-wedge-to-tam-at-tc-early-stage)
- Phenomenal Ventures' Helen Min (2023): a TAM can shift as products expand into related offerings or geographies, and a leader in a growing category can explain its role in that expansion; she wants a small initial market of obsessed customers and a TAM that can return the fund. — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/06/how-founders-should-approach-tam-when-venture-capital-is-scarce/)
- Lemkin: expansion is expected ("almost always, you'll exhaust your initial wedge") and should be evidenced by actual product/segment moves (Salesloft, Talkdesk, Algolia). — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/every-year-you-should-double-your-ttam-your-true-tam/)
- Octopus: describe the "tilt" (product has "use-cases beyond its current application") and lateral scaling with concrete analogues (Facebook from Harvard, Uber from SF limos, Airbnb into hotels). — [Octopus Ventures](https://octopusventures.com/blog/we-need-to-talk-about-tam/)
- A secondary VC glossary proposes the test "has any similar company successfully made this expansion?" and warns expansion requiring customers to change fundamental behavior is "almost always overestimated". Not a primary investor source. — [VC Beast glossary, TAM expansion](https://vcbeast.com/venture-capital-glossary/tam-expansion)

**Other called-out errors**
- Fusion VC/PSG: "Overly optimistic assumptions" about adoption/growth/price; "Ignoring disruptors"; the market must be growing (a big but flat market "is also a problem" per Woodard). — [Fusion VC](https://blog.fusion-vc.com/p/masterclass-market-sizing); [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/06/how-founders-should-approach-tam-when-venture-capital-is-scarce/)
- Radlo-Zandi: "Don't inflate your estimates, because investors will expect you to achieve these numbers." — [TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/09/how-to-calculate-your-startups-tam-sam-and-som/)
- Underscore: inconsistency between the market-size customer and the customer described elsewhere in the deck weakens the pitch. — [Underscore VC](https://underscore.vc/resources/bottom-up-market-size-slide/)

### Inferences
- Investors' objection is rarely about the number being large; it is about provenance: can the partner rebuild the number from stated customer counts and prices? Every primary source above converges on "show the derivation".
- The seats-vs-companies unit error is a special case of Dreamit's "overly broad customer counts" and Underscore's "know what one customer is worth": the count and the price must share a unit (account vs seat). This is an inference; no source states it in those words.

### Gaps
- No primary investor source was found that names the seats-versus-companies unit mismatch explicitly.
- No primary source was found that uses the phrase "TAM at today's price"; the concept is covered indirectly by Gurley (elasticity) and Dreamit (pricing models).
- Searches for "non-consumption" in a startup-sizing context returned nothing citable beyond Gurley's Uber examples and Dixon's Christensen reference.

## Key Question 4: What do investors say about ranges, sensitivity, assumptions, and sizing a market that does not exist yet?

### Takeaway
Investors ask for directional accuracy with explicit, defensible assumptions and a sensitivity range rather than a point estimate; for new categories they explicitly say the historical market is the wrong denominator and want a bottom-up build from a wedge, analogues, and evidence of latent demand (interviews, early-city data), while acknowledging their own history of passing on Airbnb-type companies because "TAM looked too small".

### Cited Findings

**Ranges, sensitivity, assumptions**
- Fusion VC/PSG: assumptions are "absolutely fine as long as your assumptions make sense and you show your work"; stress-test with a sensitivity analysis to find the range in which the opportunity still holds; cross-reference multiple sources and methodologies; be ready to defend less favorable scenarios; whether a market is "$2.5 billion or $2.7 billion doesn't make a huge difference"; the goal is "an informed, reasonable estimate"; benchmark bar roughly $1-2B "depending on who you ask". — [Fusion VC](https://blog.fusion-vc.com/p/masterclass-market-sizing)
- Senkut: "The plan doesn't have to be accurate: The plan has to be directionally correct." — [TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/04/what-investors-really-think-about-the-tam-slide-in-your-pitch-deck/)
- Herrera (Reach): there is no single correct number; "It's more about [whether] we have overlap and agreement in the assumptions." — [TechCrunch, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/06/how-founders-should-approach-tam-when-venture-capital-is-scarce/)
- Pear: state assumptions explicitly throughout; present TAM and 5-year revenue both bottom-up; market sizing "is a rough estimation, so keep it simple"; Pear values "clarity of thought and enduring ambition" over the number. — [Pear VC](https://pear.vc/market-sizing-guide/)
- Gurley models scenarios rather than a point: bearish-to-likely TAM of ~$450B-$1.3T and the implied share Uber would need for a 25x outcome (~56% bearish, ~20% likely). — [Gurley](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Sardana: it is "OK to create that slide and talk about the math behind your TAM". — [TechCrunch, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/25/index-ventures-jahanvi-sardana-shares-the-truth-about-tam-and-what-founders-should-focus-on-instead/)

**Markets that do not exist yet**
- Sardana's three buckets: "known market" (must show "why you're building a better toothbrush"), "emerging market" (e.g., non-alcoholic beer), "invisible market" ("the biggest trap", "a dark art"; 2006 smartphones: "nobody knew they wanted them and they changed the world"). "What was the market for search before Google?" Index passed on Airbnb for a too-small TAM; "Airbnb created a whole new inventory, which is now bigger than some of the largest hotel brands." For marketplaces, focus on what unlocks supply and how behavior changes once supply exists. A market description is "a lens on your ambition." — [TechCrunch, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/25/index-ventures-jahanvi-sardana-shares-the-truth-about-tam-and-what-founders-should-focus-on-instead/)
- Sequoia (current page): "Some of the best companies invent their own markets." — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- Pear: "Most successful startups create or change markets," so estimate hypothetical demand 5-10 years out, not just current demand. — [Pear VC](https://pear.vc/market-sizing-guide/)
- a16z: eBay and Airbnb "sought to address what appeared to be modest TAMs in the beginning"; existing-market size "might actually understate the opportunity of new business models". — [a16z](https://a16z.com/16-more-startup-metrics/)
- Fusion VC/PSG: "If you can't find data specific to your niche or industry, don't panic!" Use adjacent markets as a starting point; avoid assuming large shifts in technology usage within a few years. — [Fusion VC](https://blog.fusion-vc.com/p/masterclass-market-sizing)
- Shakir (Lux) recommends one-on-one customer interviews to gather pricing and behavior data for new markets; Senkut says Uber's market was underestimated because people focused on the transportation market rather than a fixed TAM; Nortman (Upfront) on a new category: "I got one investor to believe that that was a leading indicator and 99 who didn't." — [TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/04/what-investors-really-think-about-the-tam-slide-in-your-pitch-deck/)
- Reichert: "Savvy investors may prefer that startups go after a market that does not yet exist" rather than fight for share in a large established market; show "how you are going to build an ever-expanding cadre of delighted customers." — [TechCrunch, "TAM takedown"](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2443773)
- Gurley's method for a non-existent market: use the first liquid city as evidence of multiple over the historic market (3-6X in SF), then size substitution of an adjacent spend pool (car ownership) under explicit penetration scenarios. — [Gurley](https://abovethecrowd.com/2014/07/11/how-to-miss-by-a-mile-an-alternative-look-at-ubers-potential-market-size/)
- Dixon: new categories are underestimated because they "undershoot" needs at launch; the sizing question is what external forces will close the gap. — [cdixon.org](https://cdixon.org/2010/01/03/the-next-big-thing-will-start-out-looking-like-a-toy)
- Paul Graham: prefer "something a small number of people want a large amount"; "Microcomputers seemed like toys when Apple and Microsoft started working on them." — [Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)
- Altman (secondary summary): target a market that "may become a huge market within 10 years"; judge growth rate, not current size. — [Gigazine summary](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20141022-sam-altman-lecture)
- Greylock's Medium publication carries "Why 'TAM' doesn't matter to me" (author handle @mheiman; saved to Glasp Jan 28, 2021). A search summary describes its argument as: applying a strict TAM framework over the past 25 years would have led investors to pass on most of the best venture investments, and great companies expand TAM by removing friction, increasing convenience, enabling new use cases and lowering prices. The body could not be fetched (503/404), so these are unverified paraphrases. — [Greylock Medium, "Why 'TAM' doesn't matter to me"](https://news.greylock.com/why-tam-doesn-t-matter-to-me-70485c4796cf)

### Inferences
- The investor-side protocol for a new category, assembled from Gurley, Pear, Sardana and Shakir: (1) name the bucket (known/emerging/invisible); (2) size the wedge bottom-up with real counts and a price validated in interviews; (3) show early-market evidence (a liquid city, a cohort) as the multiplier over the legacy market; (4) size adjacent spend pools being substituted under explicit penetration scenarios; (5) present a range and the implied share needed for a venture outcome.
- "There is no market" is treated by investors as a claim to be tested, not a disqualifier; the standard retort is the list of companies they passed on (Airbnb at Index; eBay/Airbnb in a16z's piece).

### Gaps
- No source gives a numeric convention for how wide a presented range should be or how to label confidence; Fusion VC's "sensitivity analysis" is the most specific.
- The Greylock essay's author name, date, and exact wording remain unverified.
