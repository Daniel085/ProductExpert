## Rupert Tebb — 8 principles of effective research

1. **Start with customer need.** Begin from the user's need, not your solution.
2. **Get the right sample.** Talk to the people who actually matter for the
   question — sampling beats volume.
3. **Pay attention to people's behaviours — *they lie*.** Watch what people do;
   stated claims and opinions mislead. (Same spine as *Talking to Humans*:
   behaviour over speculation.)
4. **Examine the wider context.** Understand the surrounding situation, not just
   the isolated task.
5. **Foster empathy between the team and the user.** Research exists to build the
   team's empathy, not just to produce a document.
6. **Research is a team sport.** Involve the team; don't silo discovery in one
   person.
7. **Spend lots of time showing research.** Socialising findings is part of the
   work, not an afterthought.
8. **Cumulative, not summative.** Research compounds over time; it isn't a
   one-off verdict delivered at the end.

## Richard Edgley — what makes a good insight

> "You never really know a man until you understand things from his point of
> view, until you climb into his skin and walk around in it." — *To Kill a
> Mockingbird*

A good insight should:

1. **Be based on a first-person experience** — get into their shoes.
2. **Be an "aha" — a fresh perspective on something implicit** that's true all
   the time but goes unspoken.
3. **Be based on truth, need & tension** — being *provocative* is a good thing.
4. **Be targeted** — focus on a specific sample/segment.
5. **Suggest an idea (or ideas)** — it should point to action.
6. **It's OK to be wrong** — keep researching, experimenting & learning.

**Remember:** insights aren't an exact science — you're looking for **insights,
not proof.**

Pair these criteria with the observation → finding → **insight** → opportunity
ladder in [`talking-to-humans.md`](./talking-to-humans.md#phase-2--synthesis):
the ladder is the *structure*; Edgley's six are the *quality bar*.

## Alice Wilkie — case study: Dubai bank

Customers (Arab & Indian expats) liked the SMS notifications banks already
offered. The team turned a belief into a test:

- **We believe that…** customers would react positively to smartphone
  notifications that are personalised and timely, based on their personal data
  and behaviour.
- **To verify that…** we send personalised SMSs to customers based on their
  behaviour.
- **Built:** smart notifications — intelligent, contextual hints & tips based on
  customer behaviour to help them save money.
- **Measured:** number of opt-outs; a "wow" experience gauged via follow-up
  calls.
- **Found out that:** ~30% took the trial subscription; some customers opted
  out; **60% changed their behaviour.**

### Experiment template (reusable)

```
We believe that … (assumption about the customer / their behaviour)
To verify that … (the test we will run)
Built:          … (the minimal thing we made)
Measured:       … (the signal/metric — include a qualitative "wow" check)
Found out that: … (result → validated / invalidated / surprised)
```

## How this plugs into the agent

- **Prep** — Tebb #1–#4 reinforce framing the learning goal, sampling,
  behaviour-over-claims, and context.
- **During & after** — Tebb #5–#8: make research a team sport, show it often,
  treat it as cumulative.
- **Synthesis** — Edgley's six criteria are the **quality bar** for the insights
  step; pair with the insight ladder in `talking-to-humans.md`.
- **Next test** — use Wilkie's *We believe / To verify / Built / Measured /
  Found out* format to design the follow-up experiment.
