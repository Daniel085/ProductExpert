PRINCIPLES — Rupert Tebb (@rupert_Tebb) — what makes research effective
================================================================
#1  START WITH CUSTOMER NEED
#2  GET THE RIGHT SAMPLE
#3  PAY ATTENTION TO PEOPLE'S BEHAVIOURS  (they lie!)
#4  EXAMINE THE WIDER CONTEXT
#5  FOSTER EMPATHY BETWEEN THE TEAM & USER  (see Rupert's Medium post)
#6  RESEARCH IS A TEAM SPORT
#7  SPEND LOTS OF TIME SHOWING RESEARCH
#8  CUMULATIVE not SUMMATIVE

================================================================
WHAT MAKES GOOD INSIGHT? — Richard Edgley (@Richard_Edgley)
================================================================
Quote: "You never really know a man until you understand things from his point
of view, until you climb into his skin and walk around in it."  (To Kill a
Mockingbird)

A good insight should:
1  BE BASED ON A FIRST-PERSON EXPERIENCE — get into their shoes
2  AHA — A FRESH PERSPECTIVE ON SOMETHING IMPLICIT ALL THE TIME
3  BE BASED ON TRUTH, NEED & TENSION — being provocative is a good thing
4  BE TARGETED — focus on a specific sample
5  SUGGEST AN IDEA OR IDEAS
6  IT'S OK TO BE WRONG — keep researching, experimenting & learning

Remember that:
- Insights aren't an exact science
- We are looking for insights, not proof

================================================================
CASE STUDY — Dubai bank — Alice Wilkie (@Alice_Wilkie)
================================================================
Customers: Arab & Indian expats. "We like the SMS notifications currently
offered by banks."

EXPERIMENT
- We believe that... customers would react positively to smartphone
  notifications that are personalised and timely, based on their personal data
  and behaviour.
- To verify that... we send personalised SMSs to customers based on their
  behaviour.
- Built: smart notifications — intelligent and contextual hints and tips based
  on customer behaviour to help save money.
- Measured: number of opt-outs; a "wow" experience gauged via follow-up calls.
- Found out that: ~30% took the trial subscription; (some) customers opted out;
  60% of customers changed their behaviour.
